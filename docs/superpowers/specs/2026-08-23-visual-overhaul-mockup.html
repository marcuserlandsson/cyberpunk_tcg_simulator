Visual overhaul proposal // Cyberpunk TCG Simulator

Afterlife

A redesign that makes the simulator read as a game table in Night City, not a debug view. Built on three anchors: the official card frames we already have images of, the Cyberpunk 2077 interface language (soft red + cyan on black, angular clipped geometry, corner brackets, scanlines), and layout lessons from Gwent, Runeterra and MTG Arena. Everything below is live HTML/CSS — what you see is buildable as-is.

DIAGNOSIS

What the table looks like today

Mid-game, turn 5, captured from the current build:

  • [01]The rival's cards are rotated 90–180° and clipped off the viewport — their board is literally unreadable, you can't see what you're fighting.
  • [02]Cards are paragraphs in boxes. Full rules text is rendered at every size, so nothing has hierarchy — name, cost and power don't pop, and names visually collide with their subtitles.
  • [03]No board. Zones are labels floating in black space; the gig areas — the win condition — are a tiny text line, and legends sprawl huge and overlapping in mid-board.
  • [04]Everything is the same monospace cyan — buttons, log, card text, headings. The eye has nowhere to land.
  • [05]Dice are placeholder polygons with "?" and stats (deck, trash, eddies) are plain text chips scattered around.
  • [06]Setup, Deck Builder and Simulate are raw HTML forms — default selects and checkboxes on a black page.
DIRECTION

One visual language, from the official cards outward

The printed cards already define the aesthetic: hex cost badges, angular clipped corners, RAM-colored frames, keyword capsules, barcode strips, big power boxes. The app should feel like the surface those cards were designed to sit on.

Deck legality becomes visual: per-color RAM budget bars, a 40–50 size meter, legend slots drawn as card silhouettes. The browser uses the zoom-card rendition in a proper grid with hover states; Simulate gets the same panel chrome with results as stat blocks instead of raw text.

Motion stays purposeful and cheap: playable pulse, hover lift, a slide when cards enter a zone, dice roll flicker, and a brief red/cyan glitch flash on game-over. All CSS, all disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.

SETTLE THESE

Four decisions before I write the spec

1 · Default card rendition on the board

Official images with overlays (shown above) as the default, HTML frames as the images-off fallback — or HTML frames as the default everywhere? I recommend images-on by default: the art carries most of the premium feel and we have all 141.

2 · Hand: fan or straight strip?

The fan (in the mock) feels the most game-like; a straight strip shows more of each card at rest. I recommend the fan with hover-lift.

3 · Spent cards: stamp or rotate?

Physical TCGs rotate ("tap") spent cards; the mock uses desaturate + SPENT stamp to keep rows compact. Rotation is more traditional but costs horizontal space.

4 · Scope of motion

Baseline (glow, hover, fades) — or add showpieces: attack lunge animation, dice tumble, animated gig steal flying across the street strip? Baseline first, showpieces as a follow-up is my recommendation.
